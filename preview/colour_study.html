Colour study, not deployed

Brighter, in the S4biz family, and built on what actually converts.

Five findings from the research, one proposed system, and the same three screens rendered in the current colours and the proposed ones so they can be compared side by side. Every contrast ratio on this page is measured in your browser, not asserted.

1. What the research says

Five findings, and what each one means for this site

The popular advice ("orange buttons convert best") is mostly folklore. The evidence points somewhere less exciting and more useful.

Contrast decides clicks, not hue

There is no universally best button colour. What matters is how far the button stands out from everything around it. The famous "red beat green by 21%" test is quoted constantly, but the page was already green, so red won because it was the contrasting colour, not because it was red.

So what: pick one colour, use it for actions and for nothing else. The moment links, headings and buttons share a colour, the button stops being the loudest thing on the page and the effect disappears.

Blue is the trust default in this category

Blue and indigo dominate finance, security and business software because buyers read them as reliable and competent. That is a convention rather than a law of nature, but a convention your buyer already carries: a security product in an unexpected colour spends some of its credibility on the colour.

So what: indigo stays the spine. It is also already the S4biz brand colour, so this costs nothing and buys continuity.

Dark backgrounds punish long reading, and this site is long reading

Light text on a dark background makes the eye shift toward low-light vision, where fine detail is weaker. Readers with astigmatism see halation, a glow around letters that makes sustained reading tiring. The /partners page is roughly five thousand words.

So what: light for anything a person reads for more than a minute. Dark stays, but as a deliberate accent for the hero and evidence blocks, not the default.

On a phone, in daylight, a dark screen loses

A typical phone screen measured in a dark room shows roughly 300:1 contrast. In direct sunlight the same screen falls below 2:1. A dark page has very little contrast left to give; a light page keeps far more of it.

So what: your partners and their sellers read this on a phone, between meetings, often outdoors. That alone settles the default.

Never pure black or pure white

Pure white backgrounds cause glare and pure black causes the strongest halation. The recommended practice is off-white on soft dark, and soft off-white rather than #FFFFFF as a page canvas.

So what: the canvas below is #F6F8FC, a barely blue off-white, and the darkest text is #0C1428 rather than black.

There is a live counter-movement to dark techno-futurism

The dark-first, neon-accent look is now the default for developer products. A visible group of companies has moved the other way, to light, warm, calmer pages, specifically to read as trustworthy rather than futuristic.

So what: you sell to security officers, insurers, banks and regulators. Trust beats futurism with that audience, and going light also stops the site looking like every other security vendor.
The honest caveat. None of this predicts your numbers. These are strong priors from published work, not a measurement of your traffic. The proposal below is safe by construction (higher contrast, clearer hierarchy, one reserved action colour), but if this page ever carries real volume, the way to settle it is an A/B test on your own visitors.

3. The proposed system

Light canvas, S4biz brand, one colour reserved for actions

Two of these are darkened versions of the deck colours. #22D3EE is beautiful on a dark slide and unreadable as text on white, so it keeps its brightness on dark surfaces and has a darker twin for light ones. That is the single most common mistake when a deck palette is moved to a website.

Surfaces

Brand

Meaning

Dark, for the hero and evidence blocks only

The one rule that does the work. Indigo #4F46E5 is used for buttons and nothing else. Links are ink-coloured with an underline, headings are ink, highlights are cyan or violet. That way the only indigo on any screen is the thing you want clicked, which is precisely the mechanism the contrast research describes.

This table already earned its keep. My first draft used #6B7A94 for muted text and #0891B2 for the cyan accent. Measured, they came out at 4.08:1 and 3.68:1, both below the 4.5:1 body-text minimum. They are now #5C6B85 and #0E7490. Two failing colours would have shipped if the ratios had been asserted instead of computed.

Deliberate: bright cyan #22D3EE fails on white and is therefore never used as text on a light surface. It appears only on dark surfaces and as a solid fill behind dark text. The table proves it rather than trusting me.

6. Phone against desktop

The same palette, four rules that only apply on a phone

Raise the contrast, do not lower it

Sunlight eats contrast. Body text on a phone uses #0C1428 on #FFFFFF rather than the softer grey that is comfortable on a monitor.

Drop the tinted backgrounds

The pale indigo hero wash reads beautifully indoors and turns into flat grey outdoors. On a phone the hero is plain canvas with a coloured rule.

One button, full width

A single indigo action at least 48 pixels tall. Two buttons of equal weight on a small screen halves the click rate of each.

Colour is never the only signal

Severity carries a word as well as a colour. Roughly one man in twelve cannot separate the red from the green reliably.

7. If you approve

This is the whole change

The site already reads every colour from CSS variables in one :root block, so the switch is a replacement of that block plus a light-surface pass on the cards. No component logic changes.

/* webapp/frontend/src/styles.css, replacing the current :root */ :root{ /* surfaces, light first */ --canvas:#F6F8FC; --surface:#FFFFFF; --raise:#FBFCFE; --line:#E3E9F2; --line2:#D3DCEA; --ink:#0C1428; --body:#3A4863; --mut:#5C6B85; /* brand, from the S4biz deck */ --indigo:#4F46E5; /* ACTIONS ONLY, never a link or a heading */ --violet:#7C3AED; --cyan:#0E7490; --cyan-br:#22D3EE; /* bright: dark surfaces only */ /* meaning */ --amber:#B45309; --emerald:#047857; --rose:#BE123C; /* dark, for the hero and evidence blocks */ --d-canvas:#0E1230; --d-surface:#171B3D; --d-line:#2C3168; --d-ink:#EEF1FF; --d-mut:#A6ADD8; --grad:linear-gradient(135deg,#4F46E5 0%,#7C3AED 55%,#C026D3 100%); }
